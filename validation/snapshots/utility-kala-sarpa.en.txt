What is Kala Sarpa pattern?
Kala Sarpa (“time-serpent”) forms when all seven classical planets — Sun through Saturn — fall on one side of the Rahu–Ketu axis, the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's. Rahu becomes the serpent's head and Ketu its tail, and the planets sit ‘inside’ that arc. The pattern is sorted into twelve named types by the house Rahu occupies. Honestly stated: it is a chart geometry popularised in modern astrology rather than a rule from the classical Parashari texts, and traditions differ on how much weight it deserves.
What it means
Traditionally it is read as a life where results come after resistance — delays, then sudden breakthroughs — with strong karmic and inner themes tied to the house Rahu sits in. The balanced view matters most: countless accomplished people have this pattern. It does not doom marriage, wealth or health, and ‘partial’ arrangements (six of seven enclosed) are gentler still.
The twelve types
Named by the house Rahu occupies. Your type is highlighted once you calculate.
1. Anant
Rahu in the 1st, Ketu in the 7th. The axis runs through identity and one-to-one bonds — a strong drive to define oneself, with lessons that often arrive through partnership.
2. Kulika
Rahu in the 2nd. Emphasis on earned wealth, speech and family ties; savings that rise and dip but steady with disciplined habits.
3. Vasuki
Rahu in the 3rd. Courage, communication and initiative; success comes through persistent self-effort, siblings and short journeys.
4. Shankhapala
Rahu in the 4th. Home, mother, land and inner peace; a search for a settled base that rewards patience.
5. Padma
Rahu in the 5th. Education, children, romance and creative risk; early brilliance that matures with focus.
6. Mahapadma
Rahu in the 6th. Service, health and competition; a fighter's placement where obstacles become the training ground.
7. Takshaka
Rahu in the 7th. Marriage, partnership and public dealings; relationships are the central theme and the chief teacher.
8. Karkotaka
Rahu in the 8th. Transformation, shared resources and the hidden or occult; sudden turns that deepen resilience.
9. Shankhachuda
Rahu in the 9th. Fortune, beliefs, higher learning and father; a seeker's path that sometimes questions inherited faith.
10. Ghataka
Rahu in the 10th. Career, reputation and authority; ambition that rises through effort and occasional reinvention.
11. Vishadhara
Rahu in the 11th. Gains, networks and aspirations; large goals and wide circles, with income that grows in waves.
12. Sheshanaga
Rahu in the 12th. Foreign lands, spirituality, expenses and letting go; an inward, liberation-leaning placement.
Myths vs reality
Myth: Kala Sarpa guarantees a life of suffering or failure.
Reality: It is one pattern among hundreds; outcomes depend on the whole chart, running dashas and your own effort.
Myth: Everyone with it must do an expensive puja to be safe.
Reality: Remedies are optional and devotional — not a toll you must pay to avoid harm.
Myth: It is an ancient, universally-accepted Vedic dosha.
Reality: It is a relatively modern classification; many classical astrologers do not treat it as a dosha at all.
Traditional remedies
Devotion to Lord Shiva — the Mahamrityunjaya mantra and offering water on Mondays.
Observing Nag Panchami and honouring the serpent deities.
Steady, ethical effort in the life-area the Rahu house marks — the pattern rewards patience over panic.

A grounded perspective
If the pattern is present, read it through the strengths of the enclosed planets and the house Rahu occupies — the ‘type’ simply names the life-area it emphasises. Treat it as a theme to work with, not a sentence. For anything that worries you, a qualified astrologer reading the full chart will tell you far more than the label alone.
