What is Pitra Dosha?
‘Pitru’ means the ancestors and the paternal line. Pitra Dosha is a traditional reading that points to unfinished duties of remembrance toward one's forebears, seen through afflictions to the Sun — the significator of the father — and the 9th house of father, dharma and lineage. At its heart it is about honouring ancestors through shraddha and tarpan, not a curse laid upon you.
What it means
Where indicated, tradition connects it with themes around the father, lineage or a felt pull toward ancestral remembrance. It is best understood as an invitation to gratitude and duty — remembering those who came before — rather than a verdict of misfortune. The forms it takes
Five transparent signatures. Any that apply to your chart are highlighted.
Sun with the lunar nodes
The Sun — significator of the father — sits with Rahu or Ketu. The most-cited signature of paternal-line karma.
Sun with Saturn
The Sun joined by Saturn, planet of duty and karmic weight — read as responsibility carried down the paternal line.
Nodes in the 9th house
Rahu or Ketu occupies the 9th — the very house of father, dharma and ancestors — placing the theme at its source.
An afflicted 9th lord
The ruler of the 9th house sits with Rahu, Ketu or Saturn — the ancestral house's lord under strain.
An afflicted Sun in the 9th
The father-significator sitting in the father-house, joined by a malefic — the theme doubled at its own seat.
Causes tradition attributes
Last rites or annual shraddha for a departed elder left unperformed or incomplete.
Unfulfilled wishes or unsettled debts believed to be carried by the forebears.
Disrespect toward parents, elders or the family's ancestral duties.
Actions in earlier generations felt to affect the lineage's dharma.
Offered as tradition and belief — not as blame or fact.
Myths vs reality
Myth: Pitra Dosha means your ancestors are angry and cursing you.
Reality: It is a symbolic reminder to honour your lineage; framing it as an active curse is fear-based, not devotional.
Myth: Only costly rituals can remove it.
Reality: Sincere shraddha, tarpan and living ethically are the traditional core; elaborate paid rituals are optional additions.
Myth: There is one fixed rule everyone agrees on.
Reality: Conventions vary widely between astrologers; this page shows the specific same-sign checks it uses and applies no special aspects.
Traditional remedies
Shraddha and tarpan during Pitru Paksha, remembering ancestors by name.
Offering food or water, and charity, in the ancestors' name — feeding the needy, cows, crows or brahmins.
Caring for living elders and living ethically — held as the truest remedy of all.
Pind daan at a sacred site such as Gaya, for families who wish to.
Remedies are optional and devotional — not a fee you must pay to avoid harm.
A grounded perspective
If indications appear, the gentle traditional response is remembrance: shraddha during Pitru Paksha, tarpan, and charity or feeding offered in your ancestors' name. Take it as a prompt to honour your lineage. For personal guidance, ask a knowledgeable family elder or priest rather than reacting to the label alone.
